Core concepts
Great Circles are Lines On a sphere, the equivalent of a straight line is a great circle — any circle whose center is the center of the sphere. Just as a straight line is the shortest path between two points on a flat plane, a great circle arc is the shortest path between two points on a sphere.
Flat: straight lines extend forever in two directions
Sphere: great circles are finite loops — they always "come back"

About this demo
Great Circles A great circle is the intersection of the sphere with a plane passing through the center. These are the "straight lines" of spherical geometry — every geodesic (shortest path) lies on one.
There are infinitely many great circles, and every pair of distinct points lies on exactly one.
